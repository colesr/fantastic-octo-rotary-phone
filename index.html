01 / Premise

One name,
many shadows.

The magazines assembled for this exhibit do not offer a single Rite of Memphis. They offer accusation and aspiration: a rite called Egyptian, Misraim, Memphis; a ladder of degrees variously counted; a modern invention claimed as ancient inheritance.

Read the exhibit as an instrument for holding conflicting accounts together.

04 / Friction

The record
argues back.

The archive is not neutral. Editors repeatedly branded the Rite irregular, commercial, or imaginary; other accounts offered ancient Egyptian and Hermetic genealogies. Neither chorus is an objective verdict. Together, they show how recognition, authority, and print shaped what could count as tradition.

The supplied material includes period reports from 1860s Masonic magazines, many of them polemical. This exhibit summarizes their claims and locates their disagreement; it does not verify their assertions or reproduce ritual material.

05 / Afterimage

The name
recombines.

Modern reference accounts generally place the Rite of Memphis in Paris in 1838 under Jacques-Étienne Marconis de Nègre, and describe a later 1881 union with Misraim. The combined Memphis–Misraim name continues in several independent, differently organized bodies.

This later history does not settle the archive’s earlier disputes. It instead adds another layer: a tradition whose names, structures, and claims have changed as they moved across borders.

06 / Coda

What survives
is the question.

Names move. Counts change. Institutions draw their borders. The Memphis Rite remains, in these pages, less a fixed object than a bright disturbance at the edge of the archive.
